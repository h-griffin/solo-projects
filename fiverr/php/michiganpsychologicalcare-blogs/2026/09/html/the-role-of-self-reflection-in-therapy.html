The Role of Self-Reflection in Therapy 

Everyone goes through difficult life experiences at some point during their lives. In some cases, people might not understand why they think, feel, or behave in certain ways. Therapy provides an environment for people to slow down and reflect on their experiences and lives. Self-reflection is defined as taking time to notice thoughts, emotions, behaviors, and relationships. Therapists can help people explore their past experiences without the need for an immediate solution. Self-reflection can lead to therapy insights about a person’s patterns, needs, or values. These insights can help people move forward in the therapy process.

How Self-Reflection Can Support the Therapy Process

Therapists may ask their clients questions about their experiences to help facilitate reflection. They might ask questions to get more insight into a client’s emotions, thoughts, and behaviors. Therapists typically do not provide their clients with answers to these questions. Instead, they ask questions and give observations that may help the client consider a perspective different than their own. Some therapists practice reflective counseling with their patients. This type of technique involves therapists summarizing a client’s main feelings and words back to them. This can help clients feel validated and build empathy in the relationship.

Therapy Insights to Self-Improvement

Self-reflection gives people a starting point when they want to make changes in their lives. Through self-reflection in therapy, clients can recognize patterns and discuss possible responses with their therapist. For example, therapists can teach clients healthier coping skills or how to set firm boundaries. Self-reflection helps people improve their lives because it gives them information about what they are feeling. People can use these feelings and their personal values to make better choices based on their lives. Self-reflection provides an opportunity for people to make changes. Self-improvement is the act of making these changes. These changes usually take time and practice.

Self-Reflection Between Therapy Sessions

Self-reflection in therapy doesn’t necessarily end when the therapy appointment does. Clients can continue to reflect between sessions to reach greater insights. People can practice self-reflection in between sessions by journaling about thoughts that come up, noticing emotional reactions, and paying attention to recurring thoughts and behaviors. Clients can then bring these observations or questions to their next therapy appointment or teletherapy.

Taking the time to reflect on our experiences helps us better understand our thoughts, emotions, behaviors, and relationships. Therapy can provide a safe place for people to discuss and become curious about their experiences. Clients can use self-reflection in therapy and between sessions to make note of things they might notice. Therapists can help clients use their insights to develop a deeper understanding of themselves and provide them with tools to live happier, more fulfilled lives.
