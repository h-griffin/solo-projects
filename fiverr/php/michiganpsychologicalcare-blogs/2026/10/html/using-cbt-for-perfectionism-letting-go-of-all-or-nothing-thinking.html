Using CBT for Perfectionism: Letting Go of "All-or-Nothing" Thinking

Perfectionismcan make any accomplishment or progress feel small when it’s less than perfect. When someone struggles with perfectionism, they see all of their mistakes or setbacks as evidence of personal failing. All-or-nothing thinking is a pattern of thinking where people view situations in extremes. These extremes can make it difficult for people to recognize their progress and learn from mistakes. Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) can help people recognize their unhelpful thought patterns and create new ways to respond to mistakes. When people can learn to challenge their rigid thinking, they make room for setbacks and mistakes, which leads to learning and progress in all areas of their lives.

Understanding the Connection Between Perfectionism and All-or-NothingThinking

All-or-nothing thinking is defined as a cognitive distortion that views situations as having only two extreme outcomes. This cognitive distortion ignores the middle ground and is also known as black-and-white thinking. An example of this cognitive distortion is a person thinking. “If I miss one day of my routine, I’ve lost all my progress.” Perfectionism and all-or-nothing thinking can reinforce each other. For example, if a person sets an unrealistic standard for themselves, they will typically feel anxious about meeting those standards. If the person does not meet their standards, they view this as complete failure. They then respond to this by working themselves harder to avoid feeling like a failure. This thought loop prevents people from learning that mistakes are okay and can be a manageable part of making progress in life. Over time, this thought pattern can make it difficult for people with perfectionism to maintain motivation. This is because any setback or mistake feels like a complete failure. If a person focuses on what went wrong but never takes time to recognize what goes right, they might be missing opportunities to see their growth.

What Is Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)?

Cognitive behavioral therapy (CBT) is a type of therapy used to help people understand the connection between their thoughts, emotions, and behaviors. This type of therapy helps people recognize their automatic thoughts and how they affect their lives. CBT also helps people recognize cognitive distortions within a person’s thinking. Cognitive distortions are defined as common errors in people’s thinking. These distortions can be examined and rewritten to reflect more balanced and accurate beliefs. When someone is using CBT for perfectionism, they may practice viewing mistakes as opportunities to improve instead of personal failures. CBT also teaches people the role that behaviors play in unhelpful thought patterns. For example, if a person fears making mistakes, they may check their work repeatedly. Checking for mistakes may provide temporary relief for the person, but it also reinforces the beliefs that mistakes must be avoided no matter what. CBT can help people struggling become aware of their thoughts and how they may be affecting behaviors in their daily lives.

CBT Techniques for Overcoming Perfectionism

CBT is a helpful tool in overcoming perfectionism and any cognitive distortions a person might have.CBT for perfectionism provides tools that can help people create a more flexible mindset towards themselves and the world. 

  • Identify and Challenge Cognitive Distortions: CBT can help people become aware of their automatic thoughts that come up during stressful times. Cognitive distortions are defined as common negative thought patterns that people struggle with. Identifying these thought patterns helps people become aware of their thoughts and eventually rewrite them to be more accurate. People can try to recognize these patterns by staying aware of their thoughts when things in their lives don't go as planned. CBT doesn’t teach people to ignore mistakes but rather to view them in a different way. 
  • Cognitive Restructuring: Cognitive restructuring is the process of becoming aware of and reframing unhelpful negative thoughts. When people work on cognitive restructuring, they may want to consider the evidence for and against their thought. They can also ask if their expectations are realistic. In most cases, this is done with a therapist so that patients can work through the cognitive restructuring process with support in a safe environment. An important part of this is creating more realistic thoughts instead of trying to create an overly positive perspective. Keeping a record or journal of recurring thoughts can help people identify thought patterns that come up frequently. They also might be able to learn what triggers they have in regard to their perfectionism.

  • Set Realistic Standards: CBT can help people make realistic goals that leave room for mistakes and setbacks. This type of therapy can help people struggling with perfectionism reframe their thoughts towards mistakes and create realistic goals. Remember that working through perfectionism does not mean people are choosing to be lazy or less hardworking. They are simply working on creating realistic standards and goals that allow room for mistakes, setbacks, and imperfection. Setting realistic standards helps people make little progress consistently over time.

  • Behavioral Experiments to Challenge Perfectionism: Behavioral experiments are opportunities for patients to test their beliefs in a safe environment. Behavioral experiments can test a patient’s perfectionism by showing that rigid rules may not be necessary. Even if an experience feels uncomfortable for a person struggling with perfectionism, it’s important for them to reflect on what happened after experiments. These experiments may feel uncomfortable, but repeating the process provides opportunities for people to tolerate imperfection in small ways.

  • Tolerating Imperfection: Through behavioral experiments, people struggling with perfectionism can practice tolerating imperfection in manageable ways until they become more comfortable with it. Repeated practice helps people feel more comfortable with imperfection. Becoming comfortable with imperfection doesn’t mean a person will feel comfortable right away. It’s normal for people to continue to experience self-doubt as they work through tolerating imperfection. People can start with situations that feel more manageable for them and work their way up to more difficult situations. This helps people to not feel overwhelmed but still see progress in tolerating imperfection. Therapists can help people determine appropriate steps and pace based on each patient’s goals. 

When to Seek Professional Support for Perfectionism

When someone is struggling with perfectionism, they might benefit from professional support. If a person feels that their perfectionism interferes with their work, school, relationships, or pleasure, they may want to consider professional help. If someone is wondering if they struggle with perfectionism, there are some warning signs to look out for. For example, fear of failure, excessive time spent checking, self-critical thoughts, and difficulty completing daily tasks. Professionals can help patients identify the thought patterns and behaviors that are fueling their perfectionism.

Perfectionism and all-or-nothing thinking can make normal mistakes feel like drastic failures. CBT can help people become aware of their cognitive distortions and develop a more balanced outlook. It’s important to remember that changing these thought patterns takes time.
